ChatGPT vs Google: How Local Businesses Get Recommended

By Amir, SEO & AI Search ConsultantPublished November 25, 202630 searches · 10 industries

More people now ask ChatGPT "who's the best plumber near me?" instead of typing it into Google. So I wanted to know: does AI recommend the same businesses Google does? And if not, what decides who gets named?

I ran 30 local searches across 10 industries in one large Canadian city, on ChatGPT and on Google, on the same day, and compared every business each one recommended.

Key findings at a glance
  • Only 15% of ChatGPT's picks were also in Google's map pack.
  • In 55% of searches with a map pack, ChatGPT and Google had no business in common.
  • ChatGPT's #1 pick was in Google's map pack in only 10% of searches.
  • Rewording the question changed the answer: only 29% of ChatGPT's picks repeated.
  • Google showed an AI Overview on 100% of searches.
  • 48% of Google's top organic results were directories, forums or media.
  • Reddit appeared in Google's top results for 67% of searches.
  • Ranking well on Google Maps does not mean AI will recommend you.

1. ChatGPT and Google's map pack mostly disagree

Across the 20 searches where Google showed a local map pack, ChatGPT recommended 86 businesses. Only 13 of them were among Google's top three. ChatGPT's first pick matched a map pack business just twice.

Agreement depended heavily on the industry. For accountants, the two lists were almost identical: the same three CPA firms led both. For plumbers, dentists, real estate agents, movers and physiotherapy clinics, there was no overlap at all.

Why are the results so different?

They look in different places and rank in different ways.

  • Google's map pack ranks Google Business Profiles. It weighs how close the business is to the searcher, how relevant it is and how strong its reviews are.
  • ChatGPT ranks what the web says about you. It has mostly relied on Bing's index: it runs a web search, gathers 20 to 30 top results and organizes them with its own logic (Search Engine Land). It is now adding its own index and other sources such as Yelp and TripAdvisor (Peec AI).

So ChatGPT tends to pick businesses that are widely mentioned in "best of" lists, directories, forums and professional listings, while Google's map pack picks the strongest profile near the searcher. Where they agreed, as with accountants, a few firms were strong on both at once: lots of reviews, consistent listings and frequent mentions on industry sites.

2. Ask the question differently, get different businesses

Each industry was searched three ways: "best", "most trusted" and "affordable". Across the 10 industries, ChatGPT named 93 different businesses, and only 27 of them showed up in more than one version of the question.

  • Most consistent: personal injury lawyers and web design agencies. The same five firms came back for both the "best" and "most trusted" questions.
  • Least consistent: real estate agents. 12 different names across three questions, none repeated.
  • "Affordable" changes everything. It surfaced a different set of businesses in almost every industry, often discount or flat-fee providers.

For a business owner, this means there's no single "AI ranking" to win. You need to be recommendable for each kind of question your customers ask.

3. AI answers depend on who's asking

My first ChatGPT run was on an account with memory turned on. Even in temporary chats, the answers were personalized: ChatGPT referenced my general area and details from past conversations, and leaned toward businesses that matched. When I turned memory off and reran the same questions, only 39% of the originally recommended businesses appeared again, and 5 of 19 questions had no overlap at all.

Location mattered too. Even with memory off, ChatGPT used the approximate location of the connection and favoured nearby businesses. Two people in different parts of the city can get different "best" lists.

4. Google's AI Overview leans on the map pack and on "it depends"

Google showed an AI Overview on all 30 searches. On 4 of them it simply restated the three map pack businesses in sentence form. On 9, it declined to name a winner ("there is no single most trusted…") and pointed people to reviews, rankings or directories instead. That happened most often for "most trusted" questions.

The takeaway: for local searches, your Google Business Profile feeds the map pack and the AI Overview at the same time.

5. Directories and Reddit shape both

Nearly half of Google's top organic results were not businesses at all.

Reddit appeared in the top results for 20 of the 30 searches. HomeStars, Yelp, Clutch, RankMyAgent, Opencare and legal rankings like Best Lawyers showed up again and again.

ChatGPT relied on the same kinds of sources. It linked to RankMyAgent for real estate, Lexpert for lawyers, and the provincial licensing bodies for accountants, dentists, car dealers and HVAC contractors, and it cited Clutch review scores when ranking web design agencies.

6. Most recommended businesses don't get a link

ChatGPT included a clickable link in 17 of 30 answers, but most recommended businesses got none. Across 124 recommendations, ChatGPT linked to 22 business websites and 11 directories or regulators. Being named is the win. A visit is not guaranteed, so your name needs to be easy to find and trust when people search it next.

How to get your business recommended by AI

Based on what showed up in this study and what Google, OpenAI and independent researchers have published, here's where I'd focus:

  1. Complete your Google Business Profile and keep earning reviews. Choose the right primary category and fill in every field. Google says review count, ratings and mentions across the web in links, articles and directories all count toward local ranking.
  2. Claim your Bing Places listing. ChatGPT has relied heavily on Bing's index for local searches (Search Engine Land), so being complete and accurate on Bing matters.
  3. Let ChatGPT read your website. OpenAI recommends allowing its OAI-SearchBot crawler in robots.txt for your site to appear in ChatGPT search. Check that your firewall or hosting doesn't block it either.
  4. Build clear service and location pages. In BrightLocal's study of 800 local searches, business websites made up 58% of ChatGPT's sources.
  5. Get listed and reviewed where your industry is compared. HomeStars for trades, Clutch for agencies, Opencare for dentists, RankMyAgent for realtors, legal rankings for lawyers. Whitespark's research on ranking in ChatGPT points to Facebook and Yelp as the review sites to prioritize in most markets.
  6. Get mentioned in "best of" lists, local news and industry roundups. ChatGPT recommends businesses that other sites write about. BrightLocal found these mentions made up 27% of its sources.
  7. Make sure regulators and associations list you correctly. ChatGPT checked licensing bodies when judging trust.
  8. Earn genuine mentions in local discussions. Reddit was in Google's top results for 67% of searches. You can't fake this, but you can be helpful where your customers ask questions.
  9. Be clear about what you charge and keep your details consistent. "Affordable" questions often surfaced businesses that stated pricing or free estimates. Use the same name, address and phone everywhere.
  10. Track more than one question. Check the "best", "most trusted" and "affordable" versions of your main search on both Google and ChatGPT.

Methodology

On November 25, 2026, I asked ChatGPT and Google the same 30 local questions (10 industries, each phrased three ways: "best", "most trusted" and "affordable") and compared the businesses each one recommended. ChatGPT was tested with memory off. AI answers change from run to run, so treat this as a snapshot, not a ranking.

Why this study matters

Most businesses still measure visibility one way: where they rank on Google. This study shows that is now only part of the picture.

  • Google alone isn't enough anymore. Google is still far bigger: ChatGPT handles roughly 12% of Google's search volume and Google sends about 190 times more traffic to websites (Ahrefs). But ChatGPT is a large, fast-growing second channel, and it picks different businesses.
  • Two different shortlists. 85% of the businesses ChatGPT recommended were not in Google's map pack. A business can win on Google Maps and be invisible in ChatGPT, or the other way around.
  • Fewer clicks, higher stakes. AI gives people a short list and often no link. If you're not named, you're not considered, and you'll never see that lost customer in your analytics.
  • No single ranking to check. Answers changed with the wording, the person asking and where they were. Checking one keyword once tells you very little.
  • Google is becoming AI too. An AI Overview appeared on every search, so even classic Google results are now filtered through AI summaries.

Final thoughts

AI visibility isn't a separate channel with secret tricks. ChatGPT and Google's AI both decide who to recommend by looking at what the rest of the web says about you: reviews, directories, licensing bodies, community discussions and your own website. The one industry where both agreed, accountants, was the one where a few firms were strong on every one of those signals.

The goal isn't to choose between them. Google is still where most searches happen, and it should stay your foundation. But ranking well on Google no longer guarantees you'll be recommended by ChatGPT, so it's worth building visibility on both.

The opportunity is that most businesses haven't started. Ask ChatGPT and Google the questions your customers ask, see who gets named, and work on the signals that are missing.
